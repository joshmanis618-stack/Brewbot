SA:V — Surface Area to Volume

Oak flavor compounds (vanillin, lactones, tannins) enter the spirit through the barrel's inner surface. The more surface per liter of liquid, the faster extraction happens.

  • 53-gallon / 200 L — 75 cm²/L (industry standard)
  • 10-gallon / 38 L — ~131 cm²/L (~1.7× faster)
  • 5-gallon / 19 L — ~165 cm²/L (~2.2× faster)
  • 1-gallon / 3.8 L — ~290 cm²/L (~3.9× faster)
Temperature Multiplier

Every +10°C / +18°F above the 15°C / 59°F cellar baseline roughly doubles the extraction rate:

  • 59°F / 15°C → 1.0× (reference)
  • 68°F / 20°C → 1.4×
  • 77°F / 25°C → 2.0×
  • 86°F / 30°C → 2.8×
Bad Motivator Setup

A stainless bain-marie or food-grade drum with a solid wood lid (and optionally wood staves or spirals inside). Only the wood surface counts toward SA:V. Use the lid area calculator in the form to find your effective SA:V and see how it compares to a 53-gallon barrel.

Aging Planning

When starting a barrel aging record from a Brew Session, set a Target 55-gal Equivalent Months and the app calculates how many actual days you need based on your barrel's SA:V × temperature multiplier.

Color (SRM)

Log SRM with each tasting entry to track how the spirit darkens over time. Pale new-make spirit typically starts at 0–1 SRM; a 4-year bourbon is typically 10–20 SRM.

Wood Types
  • American Oak — vanilla, coconut, caramel
  • French Oak — spice, toast, subtle tannins
  • Hungarian Oak — bold tannins, dark fruit
  • Cherry — fruity, light tannins
  • Acacia — floral, delicate
