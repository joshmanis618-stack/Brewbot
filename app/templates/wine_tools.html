SO₂ Management

Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) protects wine from oxidation and microbial spoilage. The active fraction is molecular SO₂, which is highly pH-dependent. At pH 3.4, only ~3% of your free SO₂ is in the molecular form; at pH 3.8, only ~1%. Target 0.6–0.8 ppm molecular SO₂ for table wines.

K₂S₂O₅ (potassium metabisulfite) is 57.6% SO₂ by weight. Camden tablets each contain approximately 0.44 g K₂S₂O₅.

Acid Adjustment

Tartaric acid is the most stable and wine-native acid for additions. Citric acid can be metabolized by bacteria (use with caution before MLF). Malic acid is naturally present in grapes but also metabolized during MLF.

Chaptalisation

Adding sugar raises Brix and therefore potential ABV. Sucrose must be dissolved first. Dextrose and honey require slightly larger additions for the same Brix increase due to lower sucrose content. Always bench trial before a full addition.
